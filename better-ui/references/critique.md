# Critique against the bar

Use this to review rendered screenshots, your own or as an independent reviewer. The bar is the popular work in design communities for this kind of screen. Be as demanding as a stranger scrolling past: they give a design about two seconds.

Contents: how to review · the rubric · the usual gaps and their fixes · brief for an independent reviewer

## How to review

1. Look at the desktop first screen for two seconds. What did you notice? If the answer is "text", the showpiece is missing or weak.
2. Look at the full desktop page as a thumbnail. Is every section visually distinct, with something to look at? Where does it go flat?
3. Zoom into the showpiece. Count its designed pieces. Does it look like a real product, or like boxes?
4. Check surfaces and depth: can you tell ground, surface and raised apart? Is there a contrast surface?
5. Check the phone screenshots the same way.
6. Score each dimension, write the three most damaging gaps, fix, render again.

## The rubric

Score each 1 to 10. Anything under 8 is unfinished work.

| Dimension | 5: competent but plain | 7: good, a notch below the bar | 9: at the bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Art direction** | Pleasant, could be any product in the category | A recognisable direction, applied to colours and type but not fully to composition and visuals | A look you could name in a phrase, carried through every element, tuned to this product |
| **Showpiece** | A headline and buttons, or a simple box standing in for the product | A real product visual, but thin: few details, no lifted-out cards, no atmosphere | A detailed, believable visual with floating details and atmosphere; the first thing you see and worth a second look |
| **Richness** | Mostly text; few or no images, icons, charts or illustrations | Visuals in the first screen, sparse after it; lists without thumbnails or avatars | Something designed to look at in every section; every repeated item carries a small visual; several chart or illustration forms |
| **Depth and finish** | Flat: one shadow or none, content on the bare page | Cards and shadows present but uniform and timid | Clear levels, layered shadows with light edges, a contrast surface, consistent radii, nothing unfinished |
| **Colour** | Neutral with one colour used for everything | A decent palette, used evenly | A confident system: ground, surfaces, a concentrated accent, a category palette where items come in kinds |
| **Typography** | One size family, weak contrast between levels | Clear hierarchy, default-feeling display type | Display type with character and tight control, a strong scale, fine small labels and figures |
| **Composition** | Stacked blocks, equal weight, empty areas | Balanced but predictable; some dead space | A clear focal point, overlap and scale contrast, a full and intentional layout at every width |
| **Phone** | Desktop narrowed; visuals shrunk or dropped | Reflowed sensibly; showpiece weaker | Recomposed for a thumb; still striking |

Also confirm the floor: text is readable everywhere, nothing overflows, controls work. A page that fails the floor is not at the bar whatever it scores.

## Finishing pass

Showcase work has no loose ends. On both the desktop and the phone screenshot, read every string and look at every edge:

- **Clipped text.** No string ends in an ellipsis or is cut by its container. Widen the column, shorten the text, or allow two lines.
- **Collisions and missing spaces.** Labels that run into each other ("ordersToday"), a tag sitting on top of a legend, a caption wrapping to three lines beside a bar.
- **Covered content.** A floating card, stamp or tooltip hiding the names, figures or chart marks beneath it. Overlaps belong on margins and empty corners.
- **Gutters.** The left and right page margins are equal on the phone. Cards do not touch or nearly touch one screen edge.
- **Overlaps.** Stacked avatars and chips do not hide each other's content.
- **Empty bands.** A sidebar with a void in the middle, a column that ends far above its neighbour, a card that is half empty.
- **Legends and labels.** Every chart series has a swatch; every figure has a unit.
- **Repeats.** The same thumbnail, avatar colour or tile used again and again.

## The usual gaps and their fixes

- **"The first screen is mostly text."** Build the product visual from `showpiece.md`: an app window or object with real content, floating cards, atmosphere behind.
- **"The product mock looks like a wireframe."** Triple its density: navigation with icons, figures with deltas, a real chart, rows with avatars and status pills. Use real names and numbers.
- **"It goes flat after the hero."** Give each section its own visual: a bento of feature tiles with mini interfaces, a large demonstration, a stat band, testimonial cards with portraits, pricing cards with a featured plan, a closing band with a visual.
- **"Content sits on the bare page."** Wrap it: an app shell with sidebar and top bar, cards on a tinted ground, a dark or accent feature card.
- **"No imagery."** Thumbnails in lists, illustrated portraits for people, dimensional objects on item cards, an illustration at the heart of the hero module, more than one chart form.
- **"The imagery is generic."** One glyph recoloured for every item, initials for every person, thin line icons everywhere. Draw distinct objects and portraits (`showpiece.md`).
- **"Everything is the same."** Vary tile sizes and treatments; give categories their own hues; alternate section surfaces.
- **"Depth is timid."** Increase ground-to-surface contrast, use the layered shadow scale, add light top edges, lift the featured item.
- **"The colour is monotonous."** Concentrate the accent, add a counter-accent, add one contrast surface.
- **"The colour is a rainbow."** Too many hues at full strength. Keep one dominant accent and one counter-accent; reduce category hues to small marks.
- **"The type is generic."** Use the direction's display face, larger and tighter; add eyebrow pills, emphasised phrases, big figures.
- **"There is dead space."** Enlarge the showpiece, let elements overlap section edges, use the full width on desktop with a multi-column composition, balance column heights, size each tile's visual to fill its tile, tighten the gaps between sections.
- **"The page is monotone."** One tone from top to bottom. Add a section in the opposite tone and a tinted band, raise the contrast between cards and ground, and vary the section recipe.
- **"The phone version lost the magic."** Recompose the showpiece for narrow screens instead of hiding or shrinking it.

## Brief for an independent reviewer

When handing screenshots to a subagent, give it this and nothing about your intentions:

> You are a senior visual designer who knows what popular Dribbble shots in this category look like. Review the screenshots at [paths] of a page built for this brief: "[one line]". Read `[path]/references/critique.md` for the rubric. Look at the images, then return: a score from 1 to 10 for each rubric dimension judged against that bar, an overall score, and the six most damaging gaps as specific instructions (what, where, and what to do instead), most important first. Be demanding. Do not praise.

Act on its list in order. Then render again and ask a fresh reviewer, not the same one.
