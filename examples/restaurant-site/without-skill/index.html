Japanese kitchen · Cihapit, Bandung

Twelve seats, one counter, and whatever the highlands sent this morning.

Asagiri means morning mist. We cook Japanese food with West Java produce — vegetables from Lembang, fish from Pangandaran, tea from Ciwidey — and serve it across a cypress counter, a few plates at a time.

料理人The chef's story

Raka Wiradinata grew up in Dago, the son of a tea trader, and left Bandung at nineteen for a hospitality course in Osaka that he did not finish. A summer job washing rice at a small kappo restaurant in Kanazawa turned into eleven years.

He started at the sink and ended at the counter. His teacher, Mr. Morita, cooked for fourteen guests a night and bought fish at Omicho market every morning before six. There was no fixed menu; there was the market, the weather, and whoever was sitting down that evening. Raka spent three years on rice and dashi alone before he was allowed near a knife in front of guests.

He came home in 2019 with the idea that the method would travel better than the ingredients. Bandung sits at 700 metres, cool enough for daikon, shiso and mizuna to grow an hour up the road in Lembang. The south coast lands mackerel, snapper and squid overnight. So the kitchen flies in very little: kombu, katsuobushi, good soy. Everything else comes from within a few hours' drive.

Asagiri opened in 2021 in a converted house near Cihapit market, with a counter built from a single plank of hinoki that took eight months to arrive. It seats twelve. Raka still cooks every service, and his mother still chooses the tea.
